When players complain, they're almost always right that something is wrong. They're often
wrong about the fix. A complaint is a symptom. Your job is to find the cause.

## "Drop rates are too low"

The lazy fix is to double the drop rate. Now the item is common, the people who already got
it feel cheated, and in a month the economy is flooded.

Look at the data first. Say an item has a 1% drop chance. Half of all players get it within
about 69 runs, which sounds fine. But one player in a hundred needs over 450 runs. Those are
the people writing the angry posts, and they aren't wrong. Pure randomness has no ceiling.

So I fix the ceiling, not the rate:

- **Add a pity system.** Every miss raises your chance a little, and by a set number of runs
  it's guaranteed. The unlucky tail disappears.
- **Pick a target window.** Decide how long the item *should* take, for example most players
  within a week of normal play. Then use the metrics to nudge the base rate until the real
  numbers land inside that window.
- **Change as little as possible.** Small steps, then check the data again.

The lucky players still get it early, the median barely moves, and nobody is stuck forever.

## "The boss hits too hard"

The lazy fix is to cut its damage. Now the players who could already beat it have nothing
left to chase.

Instead, keep the old damage, call it **hard mode**, and give everyone else a way to earn
their way down. For example, hide keys or lights around the dungeon map, and have each one
give the party a defense bonus for the boss. Find them all and you're on normal mode. Skip
them and you're fighting the original boss.

Now the dungeon has more to do, good players get a challenge, and struggling players get a
plan instead of a wall. Arcana's Deserted Tomb works on the same idea: light all eight trial
rooms within a minute and the master wakes in hard mode.

## The grind should be the fun part

Games are about excitement. Grinding for gear, hitting problems and finding things that are
broken can all be part of that, if you design them in.

**Make finding bugs part of the game.** One thing I want to build: an NPC you report bugs to,
in game. We admins see every report in game too, and players who find real issues get paid
out in rewards. Bug hunting becomes something players chase, and we get better reports.

**Give the grind real highs.** A long grind works when it has moments. Occasional rare
drops that aren't exactly what you came for, but still make you stop and look, keep people
going while they chase the thing they actually want.

**Let players choose harder for better.** Higher difficulty should pay more. You clear fewer
dungeons, but each one moves you further. That rewards people who min/max their builds and
pushes them to play in groups. In Arcana, dungeons opened on the PvP island drop 20% more
loot, because you're risking more to be there.

The goal isn't to remove friction. It's to make sure the friction feels worth it.
